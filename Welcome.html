The problem

Beginners spend precious class time just remembering what comes next. So kata ends up feeling like a dance: a sequence of steps.

A danceRemembering the steps. Eyes on your feet. Thinking "what's next?"
A fightEvery move meets an attacker. Intent, balance, power.

If students know the sequence before they arrive, the sensei can spend class on technique, stance and spirit.

How it works

  1. Learn it at homeFollow each move with its count, stance, footwork and where you are on the floor.
  2. Practise hands-freeProp up your phone. It calls out each move in Japanese while you train in the living room or garden.
  3. Test yourself anywhereQuick quiz on the bus or at lunch. Remembering is what makes it stick.
  4. Bring it to the dojoArrive knowing the sequence, ready to work on form.

Try it in two minutes

  • Pick your grade on the home screen, then tap the red belt to see what's next.
  • Open Heian Shodan and step through a few moves. Watch the dot move.
  • Flip the toggle to The opponent.
  • Turn your sound on and press Start hands-free.
  • Answer a few Test yourself questions.

Who it's for

StudentsKyu grades working towards their first three gradings. Teens and adults.
SenseisLess time re-teaching the sequence. More time on form. Sensei tools are next on the roadmap.
